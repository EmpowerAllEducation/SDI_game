Accommodation or SDI?

This is the distinction people miss most often.

Accommodation

  • Changes how a student gets to the learning or shows what they know
  • Examples: extended time, audiobooks, preferential seating, a word bank, a calculator
  • Often stays in place for as long as the student needs it
  • The student still does the same learning
vs

Specially designed instruction

  • Changes the instruction itself: what’s taught, how, or how it’s delivered
  • Someone teaches a skill or strategy, often by modeling and guided practice
  • Support is planned to fade as the student becomes independent
  • Tied to an IEP goal and measured with data

Many students need both. A 7th grader with dyslexia can use audiobooks for the class novel (accommodation) and get explicit decoding instruction (SDI) at the same time.

    How it works

    Play

    1. Read a short classroom scenario from Pre-K through 8th grade.
    2. Decide: Is this specially designed instruction? Answer yes or no.
    3. Right away, you’ll see whether you were right, why, and how the scenario holds up on each of the six SDI lenses. For non-examples, you’ll also see what would turn it into SDI.

    Checks

    • Quick Check: 10 scenarios, about 3–5 minutes.
    • Full Check: 20 scenarios, about 10 minutes.
    • Presenter mode: team play for PD sessions. You control the pace.

    Nothing is timed. If you leave in the middle, your progress is saved on this device so you can pick up where you left off.
